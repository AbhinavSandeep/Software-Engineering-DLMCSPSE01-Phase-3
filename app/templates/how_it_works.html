How XPtoIRL ranks games

XPtoIRL does not use a black-box model. Every result can be traced back to a reason and a source.

1. Skill-game links

Each game in the catalogue is linked to the skills it can train. Every link has a strength from 1 (weak) to 3 (strong), a one-sentence reason and at least one source.

2. Evidence levels

  • {{ evidence_badge("A") }} weight {{ weights["A"] }}: a peer-reviewed study or meta-analysis.
  • {{ evidence_badge("B") }} weight {{ weights["B"] }}: an expert or reputable publication.
  • {{ evidence_badge("C") }} weight {{ weights["C"] }}: a community source.

Many studies examine a genre (for example action games) rather than one title. In that case the reason says so, and the link has a lower strength than a link to a game that was studied directly.

3. The score

For the skills you select, XPtoIRL adds up strength × evidence weight of each link. When at least three users have rated a game for these skills, their average rating adds up to ±0.5 points. Ties are broken by the number of sources.

Example: a strong link (3) with evidence B (0.7) and a medium link (2) with evidence B give 3 × 0.7 + 2 × 0.7 = 3.5 points.

Limits

Research on video games and skills is mixed; some studies find little transfer to everyday life. XPtoIRL shows the evidence openly so that you can judge it yourself. It does not replace professional training or advice.
